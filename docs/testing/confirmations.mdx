A tool with `requireConfirmation` parks: the cursor points at the button and waits for the person to click it. Three
things are worth a test: nothing happens before the click, the tool runs after it, and the call ends cleanly when the
person does something else.

## A complete example

The checkout button, whose tool waits for the person:

<include meta='title="checkout.browser.test.tsx"'>/_examples/testing/confirmation.browser.test.tsx</include>

Start the call without awaiting it, wait until it has parked, then act as the person. `user.confirm("Place order")`
is a trusted click on the parked control (with no name, it clicks whatever control the call waits on).
`user.moveOn()` is a trusted click somewhere else on the page.

## What each matcher checks

- `toHaveParked("place_order")`: the call reached its waiting state. Use it with `expect.poll`, since parking happens
  after the cursor has moved.
- `toBeToolResult()` on the awaited call: it ran after the click.
- `toBeToolError("cancelled")`: the person moved on, and the agent was told so. A gate nobody answers ends with
  `needs_user` after ten seconds.
- `toHaveYielded("pointer")`: the cursor got out of the way of the person. The reason is `pointer`, `keyboard`,
  `wheel` or `touch`.
